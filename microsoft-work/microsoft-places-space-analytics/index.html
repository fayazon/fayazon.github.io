01 / Microsoft Places · Case study

Make space for better decisions.

Designing the analytics experience that helps real estate and facilities leaders plan hybrid workplaces with evidence, not guesswork.

  • Zero to one
  • Enterprise analytics
  • Admin experience
  • Generally available
Role
Design lead, Space Analytics (admin experience)
Timeline
2023 – 2025
Team
Product, engineering, research and data science across India and Redmond
Platform
Microsoft Places admin experience

The problem

Hybrid work made office attendance unpredictable, and space decisions ran on manual counts and guesswork.

What I led

The analytics experience system and an insight-to-action model, from private preview to general availability.

Outcome

Shipped at general availability and showcased at Microsoft Ignite 2024.

Context

Microsoft Places helps organizations run hybrid work: when people come in, which spaces they book and how those spaces get used. Space Analytics is the part of Places that real estate and facilities teams use to understand utilization and plan their portfolio.

I led design for the admin-side analytics experience, from private preview through general availability.

The problem

Most utilization data came from manual observation and anecdotal feedback. With attendance changing day to day, facilities leaders struggled to:

  • Allocate desks and rooms when attendance swings from one day to the next
  • Find the most and least used spaces, and act on what they found
  • Justify budget and investment requests to leadership with real data

Preview customers told us something more fundamental: they would only act on numbers they trusted. The experience had to be clear about where the data came from and how it was calculated.

Who I designed for

Real estate & facilities managers

Optimize space against company policy and justify investments. Success means healthy utilization, good employee sentiment and controlled cost.

IT admins

Configure the policies, devices and signals that make spaces work. They need to know exactly what to change, and why.

Business leaders

Need the right space for their teams and the right hybrid rhythm. They shape behavior through team guidance.

Key insight: the person who spots a space problem is rarely the person who can fix it. The design had to connect them.

What I designed

I built the analytics experience as a system of four reusable building blocks, so every new report would look, behave and earn trust the same way.

Report cards

Consolidated, contextual metrics with detail on hover, drill-down into trends and anomalies, and a dedicated place for insights.

Contextual filters

Switch between counts and percentages, and control data granularity without losing context.

Error & help framework

Partial-data and status warnings, persistent error states with details, and help that starts in context before it goes general.

Actionable insights

Starts with rule-based insights and recommendations, with a defined path to Copilot-generated insights and a Copilot entry point.

From insight to action

A chart that shows empty rooms doesn’t free up a single room. I designed end-to-end flows that take an insight to the person who can act on it, and tested them with preview customers using interactive prototypes.

Raise workplan adoption

Workplans, where people share when they plan to come in, are one of the strongest signals for predicting office attendance.

  1. Spot it

    A facilities admin sees an insight that workplan adoption is low.

  2. Route it

    She drills into adoption by leader and requests action without leaving the report.

  3. Act on it

    The leader updates team guidance in Places and asks their managers to encourage planning.

Free up rooms nobody uses

No-show bookings waste rooms and frustrate people who are looking for space.

  1. Spot it

    Analytics finds rooms with repeated no-shows and recommends auto-release.

  2. Route it

    The facilities admin selects those rooms and sends IT a pre-filled request with the room list and setup guidance.

  3. Act on it

    IT turns on auto-release, and rooms nobody checks into go back into the pool.

How I led

  • Ran 10+ research and feedback calls with enterprise preview customers and turned what we heard into roadmap input.
  • Served as the India design point of contact for a large enterprise customer during preview.
  • Logged and triaged 100+ UX quality issues so the generally available experience met the craft bar.
  • Influenced the choice of charting framework the analytics experience is built on.
  • Built the demos and stories the team used with customers and leadership.

Key decisions

Trust before insight

Why
Customers wouldn’t act on numbers they couldn’t explain, so data sources, partial data and calculations are always explicit.
Trade-off
More status and help UI. A layered help model, in context first, kept it calm.

Every insight ends in an action

Why
An insight is only useful if the right person can act on it.
Trade-off
Flows cross personas and admin tools outside Places, so every hand-off had to carry its context.

Rules first, Copilot next

Why
Rule-based insights could ship sooner and be trusted, and Copilot insights build on the same framework.
Trade-off
Less flexible at launch, in exchange for a clear path to grow.

Outcome

  • Space Analytics moved from private preview, through public preview, to general availability, and was showcased at Microsoft Ignite 2024.
  • The report card, filter, error and insight frameworks gave the team a reusable foundation for new reports.
  • Feedback from preview customers fed directly into the product roadmap.

Admin products earn adoption through trust. The most important design work wasn’t the charts. It was making the data honest about its limits, and making every insight end with someone doing something.
