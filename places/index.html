01 / THE CONTEXT

Hybrid work changed
the planning question.

Organizations had buildings, floors, rooms, and desks. Understanding how those spaces were being used was a different problem.

Workplace decision makers needed to understand where demand and capacity might be misaligned, how usage changed over time, and which patterns warranted investigation.

The opportunity was to make the information interpretable: establish what deserves attention, show the supporting context, and help people narrow their investigation.

ATTENTION

Where should I look?

Surface a meaningful pattern before asking someone to analyze every metric.

EXPLANATION

Why does it matter?

INVESTIGATION

05 / THE DESIGN TRADEOFF

Comprehension before
analytical density.

Too little context makes a metric meaningless. Too much detail makes every decision an analysis project.

The framing is progressive disclosure: begin with what is happening, then expose significance, location, and supporting dimensions. A concise signal and inspectable evidence serve different needs—and need to stay connected.

MEANING

Define the metric.

Explain what is measured and what it is being compared against.

SCOPE

Keep context visible.

Carry the selected place and period through the investigation.

TRUST

Show the limits.

Avoid presenting incomplete observations as certain conclusions.

How I would evaluate it

Can someone understand a signal, find the evidence, and identify a useful next investigation? Task comprehension, successful drill-down, and repeat use would help assess that experience alongside broader product adoption.

The value of an analytics experience is how much work it removes from understanding the data.
